### 5.2 Why `on-release` is the discriminating value

1. **The grammar admits it and no other symbolic term exists.** `CORPUS.md:105`: "`recheck` — ISO date or
   cadence (`90d`, `180d`, `on-release`)". `scripts/check-sources.mjs:322` validates
   `isIsoDate(recheck) || /^[0-9]+d$/ || recheck === 'on-release'`. There is no finer-grained symbolic
   cadence to invent.
2. **It is the only cell value any instrument reads.** `scripts/check-release-pins.mjs:19-21` defines its
   population as "every corpus/SOURCES.md registry row (0-3 leading spaces) whose recheck cell is
   `on-release` and whose source cell carries a Chromium Gitiles tag `refs/tags/<A.B.C.D>/` older than
   --stable"; `:178` is that filter in code, and `:319` is a self-test case proving a `90d` row is not
   counted.
   `grep -rn '90d\|180d\|365d' scripts/*.mjs scripts/*.sh` finds day cadences **only** in fixtures and
   self-test rows: no gate computes a due date. A `90d` cell is prose a human may or may not honour; an
   `on-release` cell produces a verdict on every run.
3. **It fires on the only event that can change the bytes.** The source lives at a tag; nothing but a new
   release can touch it. The 154→156 delta is that event happening, and it is instructive: the enabling
   path held while three of six cited line ranges moved. With the receipt committed the tripwire is live
   and computed — deleting one enabling line from the 156 bytes (`bidi_cmd.Set("method", "session.new");`)
   and keeping the receipt/refetch digests consistent with the edited file makes the gate read
   `S125 verdict=drifted reasons=["s1-drift-unsuperseded"]`, exit 1 (control C3b below; C3 shows the same
   edit caught one step earlier as `f6-refetch` when the digests do not follow).
4. **Its cost is explicit and it cannot land alone.** Flipping the cell without the companions is red:
   `population 24, resolved 23, unresolved 1`, `S125 reasons=["r1-entry missing or no files"]`, exit 1
   (control A). That is the discriminating property a date cannot have: the change is self-checking, and
   a checker enforces the evidence that justifies it.

Round 33's alternative ("record a reason for `90d`") is not defensible any more: its supporting quote was
the *runtime entry's* recheck, not the registry cell, and the spot-check withdrew it for that reason
(`corpus/lessons/round-33.md:144-151`, `corpus/lessons/round-33-verifier-spotcheck.md:153-175` item 6).

## 7. Findings about the carried round-33 draft (`draft-3-s125.md`)

1. **Byte sizes were envelope sizes.** The draft lists 90,944 B and 92,700 B as "body bytes" and in
   "Files to commit". Those are the base64 `?format=TEXT` envelope sizes; the decoded blobs are
   **68,207 B** and **69,523 B**. `f3-bytes` hashes the committed file, so the draft's *files* are right
   only if the decoded bytes are committed, and the draft's prose numbers would mislead a later
   supersession that copies them. Class: `number-drift` (low, prose only).
2. **Its convention count is stale at this freeze.** 33 tag-pinned rows, 30 `on-release`, 3 `90d`
   (S125, S127, S129) — so "30 of 31, S125 the only exception" no longer distinguishes the row. The
   change stands on §5.2 instead. Its verification rev `beec3c0a…` is a scratch commit absent from the
   lane repo (`git show beec3c0a:corpus/SOURCES.md` → "exists on disk, but not in"), so the 31/30 figure
   cannot be re-derived from the lane; mine is from the freeze.
3. **Its `f6` gap is closed by this seat.** The draft disclosed "my two role names came from one
   process". My 156 fetch (19:47:50Z) is a different process, seat and time, with the same digest; C1
   shows the gate still fails a same-seat record, so the pass is not vacuous.
4. **Range `378` is weak evidence** in both the row and any receipt: the normalised line occurs five
   times in the 156 file. Not a defect (the other five ranges pin the location), but a closure that
   samples only `378` proves nothing.

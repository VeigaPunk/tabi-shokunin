## What this does and does not establish

DOES establish: the egress-count clause contributes NO independent refusal.
With the byte-pin layer satisfied, a producer that zeroes its counts at the
source is ADMITTED by the whole chain — the wrapper signs it, the witness
attests it, the counter scores it predicate true with zero findings, and the
challenge genuinely verifies under the wrapper's own per-invocation key. So
"the egress clause contributes no independent refusal" is a statement about
the FROZEN CHAIN, not only about a hand-written receipt.

DOES NOT establish (and is not claimed): a kill-rule trigger. The byte-pin is
what stops the mutated bytes in the frozen chain — the wrapper refuses
producer-digest-mismatch at stage 1, before the browser spawns. W3 only
reaches exit 0 because the pins were RE-BLESSED to the mutated bytes, which
is an out-of-model change to the pins. Hence: a control-set calibration gap
(F8), not an in-model do-nothing reach.

DOES NOT establish anything about F1: F1 is a different and stronger class.
There the DOCUMENT is the forger's (the measure record, its public key and
the challenge answer all come from the forger), so the chain admits bytes with
no browser run and no honest producer at all. F8 is about a receipt whose
fields are wrong; F1 is about the provenance of the authenticating document
itself.

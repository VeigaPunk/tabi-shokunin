Their two caveats, recorded rather than smoothed over:

  (b) the claims pins were RE-BLESSED to each tree's producer bytes — an out-of-model change to

      copy keeps the byte-pin holding.

## Relation to F1

Different and stronger class. Here the key is genuinely the wrapper's own, the challenge genuinely
verifies, and the browser genuinely ran — what is wrong is that a receipt's FIELDS are wrong and
the clause that would have spoken about them cannot tell. In F1 the DOCUMENT ITSELF is the
forger's: the measure record, its public key and the challenge answer all come from the forger,
so the chain admits bytes with no browser run and no honest producer at all.

## ADDENDUM — my no-egress run is a CONTROL for the peer's differential

The peer has taken my non-reproduction as a third data point, and it is worth stating why it
helps rather than merely hedging. My pristine-arm run used the SAME mirror construction as the
peer's refused arm (own tree, pins blessed to that tree's own producer bytes, real wrapper, real
per-invocation key, real Firefox, real witness, real fd3/fd4 challenge) and it scored GREEN with
zero findings — because the host produced no non-loopback egress in that window.

That makes the mirror construction itself an exonerated variable: the construction does not
manufacture refusals. So the refusal in the peer's PRISTINE arm is attributable to the 45 genuine
non-loopback observations and to nothing incidental to the mirror tree — the egress clause spoke
because there was egress to speak about. Symmetrically, the ADMITTED arm in the same window is
attributable to the producer's behaviour (zeroed counts) and to nothing else, since the two arms
share the construction, the chain, the key discipline and the challenge discipline.

Practical consequence for the lane: the live arm is a CONFIRMATION of the deterministic
receipt-level paired differential, which is the form to re-run, because the egress window on this
host is intermittent and not reproducible on demand. The peer's report now says exactly this.

4. THE QUOTE (verbatim, from the fetched PDF)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIMARY QUOTE — 25 words, one sentence.
Section 6.2.2 "SHA-256 Hash Computation", printed page 23 (PDF page 28).

  "After repeating steps one through four a total of N times (i.e., after processing
   M(N)), the resulting 256-bit message digest of the message, M, is"

Fetched-line anchors (whitespace-normalised join of the two printed lines; the sentence is
line-wrapped across the page):
  - pdftotext extraction, .ufo/waves/round-65/w1/R65Scout/sources/fips180-4.pdftotext.txt
    lines 1095-1096:
      1095: After repeating steps one through four a total of N times (i.e., after processing M(N)), the resulting
      1096: 256-bit message digest of the message, M, is
  - independent second extraction of the same PDF bytes (markit reader), artifact line 835:
      "After repeating steps one through four a total of *N* times (i.e., after processing
       *M<sup>(N)</sup>*), the resulting 256-bit message digest of the message, *M*, is"
  The two independent extractions agree word for word. The sentence is continued on
  printed page 23 by the formula "H0(N) H1(N) ... H7(N)"; the words quoted above are the
  complete sentence text preceding that formula.

Why this sentence carries the predicate (message -> fixed-length digest):
  - input: "the message, M"
  - output: "the resulting 256-bit message digest" — fixed length, 256 bits
  - it is the SHA-256 hash computation (section 6.2.2), i.e. it is about SHA-256 itself.

SUPPORTING QUOTE (domain of the input, 21 words, one sentence) — same standard, immediately
preceding section, needed to show the input is a bit string:
Section 6.2 "SHA-256", printed page 21 (PDF page 26):

  "SHA-256 may be used to hash a message, M, having a length of l bits, where 0 <= l < 2^64."

Fetched-line anchors:
  - pdftotext extraction lines 1014-1015:
      1014: SHA-256 may be used to hash a message, M, having a length of l bits, where 0 <= l < 2^64 . The
      1015: algorithm uses 1) a message schedule of sixty-four 32-bit words, 2) eight working variables of 32
    (the "<=", "<" and "l" are the PDF's Symbol-font glyphs U+F0A3/U+F03C/U+F06C, rendered
     here as ASCII for the record; the markit extraction of the same line, artifact line 800,
     reads "SHA-256 may be used to hash a message, M, having a length of l bits, where
     2^64 > l." with 2^64 set as a superscript — the two extractors agree on the wording
     and differ only in glyph encoding and line-wrap placement.)
  - PDF page 26 footer prints "21"; PDF page 28 footer prints "23" — the printed-page numbers
    used above are the document's own, not the PDF's sequential index.

Two further facts, quoted exactly where they are used to defend "fixed length":
  - printed page iv (PDF page 4), Federal Information item 3: "The message digests range in
    length from 160 to 512 bits, depending on the algorithm." (SHA-256 is the 256-bit entry:
    "Figure 1: Secure Hash Algorithm Properties", printed page 3 / PDF page 8, row SHA-256:
    Message Size (bits) < 2^64, Block Size 512, Word Size 32, Message Digest Size 256.)
  - printed page 4 (PDF page 9), section 2.2.1 Parameters: "M  Message to be hashed." and
    "l  Length of the message, M, in bits."
